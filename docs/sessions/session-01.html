🎧 Podcast Episode

🎙️

Session 1 — What Are We Bringing Into the Room? ~30 min · Download and listen before or after your session

⬇ Download MP4 — Session 1

A conversation about why this class exists, what we’re trying to build, and what it means to bring your whole, complicated self into a room like this one.

Overview

The goal of Session 1 is not to cover content. It is to become a room where honest conversation is possible.

If people leave tonight thinking:

“These are people I might be able to be honest with. My questions are welcome here. We’re going to examine this seriously rather than just complain — and maybe there’s something about church worth rediscovering.”

…then Session 1 succeeded — even if you barely got to any teaching.

The arc for tonight:

Connect → Create safety → Examine our lenses → Name our experience

Resist doing much teaching until you’ve accomplished the first two.

Before You Arrive

Warning

🗒️ Facilitator prep — do this before Session 1

  • Room setup: Chairs in a circle, not rows. Name tags. Tissues within reach (not dramatic — just practical). Post the covenant where it will be visible.
  • Materials needed: Index cards (2 per person), markers, large sticky notes or whiteboard, one printed handout per person.
  • The Dress: Pull up the viral dress image on a laptop or phone you can show easily. Don’t explain it ahead of time.
  • Preaching clip: Choose a 3–5 minute clip of a preacher whose style will land differently for different people in your group — someone known for emotional intensity works well. Have it queued and ready.
  • Your own answer: Before the session, write down your own honest answer to: “What is my complicated relationship with the church?” You’ll go first in the opening. Don’t prepare a tidy answer.

1. Welcome & Framing (0:00–0:08)

Keep this under 8 minutes. Do not give a syllabus speech. Do not over-explain. Do not over-promise safety.

Say something like this — in your own words:

“This class starts with an assumption: church is complicated.

Some of us have experienced tremendous goodness through churches. Some of us have experienced real hurt. A lot of us have both stories — sometimes about the same church, sometimes the same season.

I’m not interested in defending the institution. I’m also not interested in tearing it down. I want us to understand it — what it was supposed to be, what it became, why some of it stopped working for a lot of people, and what a faithful version of it might look like.

But before we talk about church, I think we need to become a little bit of a community ourselves. So let’s start there.”

Then move immediately into activity. Do not lecture.

Note

🗒️ Facilitator note: The opening’s job is to signal: this will be serious and honest, but not heavy from the first minute. Then get them talking to each other as fast as possible.

2. Two Truths and a Lie (0:08–0:25)

Why start here? Because the alternative — “Tell us your name and why you’re cynical about church” — is too vulnerable, too soon. Two Truths and a Lie gives the room permission to laugh together first. That is not separate from the work of this class. It is the work.

Instructions:

“We’re going to start with Two Truths and a Lie. Think of three statements about yourself — two true, one false. Make them things we wouldn’t necessarily know about you. Weird jobs, unusual experiences, strange places you’ve been, famous people you’ve met — whatever gets a reaction. You’re going for laughter and surprise.”

Give 2–3 minutes to think. Then go around the room.

For group size:

  • 10 or fewer: whole group works easily
  • 11–20: split into groups of 5–7, then do a brief share-back (“What was the best lie in your group?”)
Note

🗒️ Facilitator note: Go first yourself. Choose statements that are genuinely surprising — model that being a little vulnerable is okay here. After the game, don’t explain it or draw a moral from it. Just move on. The room is warmer now. That’s enough.

3. Hopes & Curiosities — Index Cards (0:25–0:40)

Give everyone two cards.

Card 1:

I hope this class will…

Card 2:

Something I’m curious about is…

Ask them to fill these out anonymously. Collect them, shuffle, and read them aloud. Don’t comment on each one — just read. Let them hear each other.

After reading, cluster what you heard into themes on a whiteboard or sticky notes:

  • Understanding church history / what church was supposed to be
  • Processing painful experiences
  • Finding language for what happened
  • Figuring out if faith still makes sense
  • Asking questions I can’t ask elsewhere
  • Finding some kind of hope

Then say:

“This is actually going to shape where we go. I’ve planned a direction, but I want this class to respond to the questions we’re actually carrying. And I think I just heard them.”

Note

🗒️ Facilitator note: This exercise does two things. It gives people ownership — they shaped the class. And it shows each person that they are not alone in their questions. Both are powerful. Save the cards. Refer back to them in later sessions.

4. Build the Covenant Together (0:40–0:55)

Do not present the ground rules on a slide. Let the group generate them.

Ask:

“If we’re going to have conversations about things that matter deeply to us — and sometimes disagree — what do you need from this room in order to be honest?”

Write their answers as they speak. They will generate most of what’s needed:

  • Confidentiality
  • Don’t interrupt
  • Don’t judge
  • Don’t try to fix people
  • Disagree respectfully
  • Don’t dominate
  • Assume good intent
  • Be honest

Add what they miss. The two most important things to specifically name:

Confidentiality: “Stories stay. Lessons leave.” You can share what you’re learning. You cannot share who said what.

The right to pass: “Nobody has to share. Silence is participation too. You will never be called on, pushed, or guilt-tripped into disclosing something you’re not ready to share.”

Read the final list back together. Ask for verbal affirmation:

“Can we agree to try to hold these — not perfectly, but sincerely — for the next eight weeks?”

Wait for a real yes from the room.

Note

🗒️ Facilitator note: A covenant the group built is one the group owns. A list someone handed them is a policy. The process of creating it together matters as much as the content.

BREAK (0:55–1:05)

Hard stop. Do not try to sneak in content. Do not use the break for sidebar pastoral conversations — let the room breathe and let people talk to each other.

Tip

🗒️ The break is not lost time. For what you’re trying to accomplish, the break may be one of the most productive ten minutes of the evening. People will start talking honestly with each other. Let them.

5. The Dress — Introducing Lenses (1:05–1:20)

Show the viral dress image — the one that famously appeared either blue-and-black or white-and-gold depending on the viewer.

“What color is this dress?”

Let them argue. Let them laugh. Don’t resolve it yet.

Then say:

“Here’s the interesting thing. Everyone in this room is looking at the same image. The light hitting your eyes is the same. But what your brain does with that light — the interpretation — depends on things you can’t fully control: the context around it, what colors you saw just before, how your visual cortex is wired.

We call these priors. What you already bring shapes what you see.

Now: none of you walked into this room tonight without a prior history with church.”

Let that land. Then move on. Don’t turn it into a 15-minute lecture.

Note

🗒️ Facilitator note: The important sentence is: “We don’t experience reality without interpretation.” Say it once. Don’t over-explain it. The preaching clip exercise coming next will do the application work — trust that sequence.

6. Preaching Clip + Response Exercise (1:20–1:45)

Play your chosen clip (3–5 minutes of a recognizable preacher — someone with a distinct, emotionally intense style that people will respond to differently).

Step 1 — Individual response (2 min)

Show these six words and ask everyone to write down 2–3 that describe their reaction:

Inspiring · Authentic · Emotional · Performative · Manipulative · Coercive

Don’t make them raise hands yet. Give them a moment to know what they actually think before the loudest person speaks.

Step 2 — Pairs (3 min)

“Compare your reaction with the person next to you.”

Step 3 — Whole group

Invite sharing. Let the range of reactions surface. You’ll find real disagreement in the room — some found it moving, some found it manipulative, some felt both.

Then ask:

“Why might two reasonable people experience the exact same three minutes completely differently?”

Introduce this simple framework — write it where they can see it:

What happened → What I bring → How I interpret → How I respond

“Every experience of church is filtered through the middle two steps. And those are shaped by everything that came before — family, experiences, wounds, expectations. That’s not a flaw. It’s just how humans work. And it’s why two people can be in the same church for twenty years and have completely different accounts of what it was like.”

Note

🗒️ Facilitator note: The individual → pair → group sequence is important. Quieter people need to formulate what they think before the biggest personalities start talking. Protect that sequence.

7. “Church Is…” / “I Wish Church Were…” (1:45–1:57)

Give everyone two more index cards.

Card 1: > CHURCH IS… > When you hear the word “church,” what immediately comes to mind? Not what should come to mind. What actually does.

Card 2: > I WISH CHURCH WERE…

Collect anonymously. Post them on opposite sides of the room. Read them all aloud without comment or correction.

You’ll hear something like this:

Tip

CHURCH IS… judgment · Sunday · sermon · family · hypocrisy · community · music · politics · Jesus · rules · friends · boring · comfort · pressure · obligation · home

I WISH CHURCH WERE… safe · honest · loving · diverse · meaningful · welcoming · challenging · real · generous · less political · more Jesus-centered · community · a place I could ask real questions

Then say:

“Don’t correct anything on either wall. Just let the contrast sit there for a second.”

Pause for 15–20 seconds of silence. That silence is doing important work.

Note

🗒️ Facilitator note: Resist the temptation to say “Great. Now let’s spend 25 minutes on ekklesia.” The contrast between those two walls is the most powerful thing in the room right now. Don’t fill it. The “I wish” wall is revealing the positive vision underneath the cynicism. That’s the thing this whole class is going to be working with. Let people feel it before you name it.

8. Closing Thought (1:57–2:00)

Brief. Honest. Not tidy.

“Look at that ‘I wish’ wall. That’s not cynicism. That’s longing. And longing means the thing you’re longing for still matters to you.

That’s what we’re going to spend the next seven weeks with — not defending the church as it is, and not dismissing it either. But taking seriously both what it’s done and what it could be.

Come back next week. We’re going to start looking at the lenses — the priors — that shape what each of us sees when we walk into a church. It turns out that’s more interesting, and more useful, than it sounds.

Thanks for being here.”

Seed Question — Carry This With You

Tip

For the week ahead:

Pay attention this week to moments when you react to something related to the church — a news story, a conversation, a memory, a service. When you notice a reaction:

  1. Name what happened (the external event)
  2. Notice what you brought to it (your prior)
  3. Notice how those two things produced your interpretation

You don’t have to do anything with what you find. Just watch it.

After the Session

Warning

🗒️ After you leave:

  • Take 10 minutes to write down anything that felt unresolved or surprising in the room.
  • Note any individuals who may need pastoral follow-up.
  • Save the index cards — all four sets. They’re data about this group that will be useful in later sessions.
  • Send a brief message to the group within 24 hours — thank them for showing up and restate the seed question.
  • Debrief with yourself: Did you talk too much? Did you protect the quiet people? Was the room warmer at the end than at the beginning? What will you do differently next week?
